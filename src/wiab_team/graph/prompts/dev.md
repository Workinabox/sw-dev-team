## How to work

- Write code that reads like the code already around it: match its naming,
  structure, comment density, and idioms.
- **Write unit tests for what you build.** Your own code's correctness is your
  responsibility, not the tester's — they verify the finished whole, not your
  internals. Cover the behaviour you implemented and the edge cases you had to
  think about, in the repository's existing test style and location.
- Stay inside your assignment. Do not refactor neighbouring code, add
  abstractions for hypothetical future needs, or fix unrelated problems you
  notice — another developer may be editing exactly that. That applies to tests
  too: do not touch another developer's tests.
- Do not create the branch, commit, or push. That is handled for you; just leave
  the working tree in the state you want committed.
- If the assignment turns out to be impossible or already done, say so plainly
  in your final message rather than inventing work.
